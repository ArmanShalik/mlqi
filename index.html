// 02Field definitions
DATEWhich day this row covers▶
One row per calendar day. Don't fill it in from memory a week later — log it the same night.
SUBJSubject(s) studied▶
C.M (Combined Maths), Phy (Physics), Che/ICT (Chemistry/ICT). If you studied more than one subject, note all of them — the questions and hours fields below should reflect the combined total for the day, all in the same row.
PP / MPPast paper & model paper▶
Enter the percentage of the paper you completed — not a yes/no. Finished 70% of a past paper? Enter 70, not 100 and not 0. Didn't touch a paper type today? Leave it at 0.
QUESMCQ, S/SE, E▶
MCQ — attempted, out of 100.
C.M days — Short (out of 32) + Structured-Essay (out of 8), combined out of 40.
Phy/Che days — SE only, out of 16. This is automatically scaled to the same 40-point equivalent as the C.M field, so neither subject group is penalised for a different question format.
E — essay questions attempted, out of 20.

Mixed day? Studied C.M and Chemistry the same day — fill in both S/SE fields; the calculator combines them, capped at the 40-question equivalent.
CORRCorrect — optional, new in 2.0▶
The number of attempted questions you got right, self-marked against the answer key. Leave blank on days where marking isn't practical — common for S/SE/E. When filled in, it's the single largest factor in your score (35%). When blank, the score is calculated in fallback mode instead — see REV 03.
HRSTotal hours▶
Total hours actively studied, combined across all subjects.
Watch out
Hours on their own earn nothing in MLQI 2.0. They only count as the denominator of your pace (questions ÷ hours). Sitting at the table without producing output does not raise your score.
RESPens & sheets▶
Pens — 1 if you fully finished a pen that day, 0 if not. Sheets — number of sheets filled, out of a soft cap of 60. Both are minor consistency checks, not something to optimise for.
SIGNMLQI & Sign▶
MLQI is calculated automatically once the row is filled in — use the calculator, don't work it out by hand. Sign confirms the row is an honest record of the day.
// 03How the score is calculated

MLQI 2.0 runs in one of two modes depending on whether Correct was logged.

Full mode — Correct is filled in
MLQI = (0.08 × PP%) + (0.07 × MP%) + (0.25 × Questions/160) + (0.35 × Correct/Questions) + (0.20 × Efficiency) + (0.05 × Resources)
Fallback mode — Correct is left blank
MLQI = (0.10 × PP%) + (0.08 × MP%) + (0.40 × Questions/160) + (0.32 × Efficiency) + (0.10 × Resources)
TermHow it's worked out
QuestionsMCQ + (C.M S/SE, or Phy/Che SE scaled to 40) + E, capped at 160.
EfficiencyActual pace (Questions ÷ Hours) vs. expected pace of 10/hr. Capped at 100% — faster than expected doesn't add extra credit.
ResourcesAverage of (Pen: 1 or 0) and (Sheets ÷ 60, capped at 100%).
Worked example
Full past paper, half a model paper, 120 questions in 10 hours, 90 correct, one pen finished, 40 sheets used → MLQI 2.0 ≈ 80.7 / 100. Try it in the Calculator tab.
// 04Why it's weighted this way
Accuracy carries the most weight — 35%▶
A quality index has to measure quality, not just activity. Attempting 160 questions and getting most of them wrong isn't the same as attempting 160 and understanding them.
Questions attempted still matters a lot — 25% / 40%▶
Volume of practice is still the strongest single predictor of exam readiness — you can't get accuracy credit for questions you never attempted.
Efficiency, not raw hours — 20% / 32%▶
The original MLQI gave direct credit for hours logged, so a student who took 10 hours to do what another does in 4 scored higher on that term alone. MLQI 2.0 measures pace instead — questions per hour, against an expected pace of 10/hr, capped at 100%. This directly targets sitting at the desk without doing the work.
Papers use fractional completion, not a flag▶
Finishing 30% of a paper is credited fairly instead of scoring the same as not starting it at all.
Resources are small and capped on purpose — 5% / 10%▶
Pens and sheets are easy to log but easy to inflate — writing bigger or messier burns through both without reflecting more real work. Kept as a light check, not something worth optimising.
// 05How to fill in your daily log
  1. Fill in one row at the end of each day — don't reconstruct it from memory later.
  2. Combine every subject you studied that day into the same row.
  3. Enter PP and MP as a percentage completed, not a tick.
  4. Enter MCQ, the matching S/SE or SE field, and E as raw counts attempted.
  5. If you can self-mark against an answer key, fill in Correct. If not, leave it blank.
  6. Enter total hours actively studied, combined across subjects.
  7. Enter Pens (1 or 0) and Sheets used.
  8. Switch to the Calculator tab to get your MLQI — no need to compute it by hand.
  9. Sign the row.
A note on honesty
MLQI is a tool to help you see your own patterns over a month — not a number to perform for. Logging accurately, including on weak days, is what makes it useful. A low score with an honest signature is worth more than an inflated one.
